As a first-year B.Tech student aspiring for the ISRO exam, it's important to build a strong foundation in your coursework alongside your exam preparation. Here's a suggested order for reading the recommended books while aligning with your academic progress:

"Introduction to Algorithms" by Thomas H. Cormen, Charles E. Leiserson, Ronald L. Rivest, and Clifford Stein

Start with this book to build a solid understanding of fundamental algorithms and data structures. It's essential to have a strong grasp of these concepts as they form the basis for many other topics in computer science.
"Programming in C" by Stephen G. Kochan

Once you have a good understanding of algorithms and data structures, delve into learning the C programming language. This book will help you grasp the basics of programming, including syntax, control structures, functions, and pointers.
"Computer Organization and Design: The Hardware/Software Interface" by David A. Patterson and John L. Hennessy

Next, move on to understanding computer architecture and organization. This book will provide insights into how hardware and software interact, which is essential knowledge for understanding system-level programming and optimization.
"Operating System Concepts" by Abraham Silberschatz, Peter B. Galvin, and Greg Gagne

Once you have a basic understanding of computer architecture, delve into operating system concepts. This book will introduce you to key concepts such as processes, memory management, file systems, and concurrency.
"Computer Networks" by Andrew S. Tanenbaum and David J. Wetherall

After gaining knowledge in operating systems, move on to studying computer networks. This book covers topics such as network architecture, protocols, TCP/IP, and network security, which are crucial for understanding communication systems.
"Database System Concepts" by Abraham Silberschatz, Henry F. Korth, and S. Sudarshan

Once you're familiar with computer networks, dive into database management systems. This book will introduce you to relational database concepts, SQL, transactions, and database design principles.
"Software Engineering: A Practitioner's Approach" by Roger S. Pressman and Bruce Maxim

As you progress, it's essential to understand software engineering principles. This book will cover topics such as software requirements, design, testing, and project management, which are crucial for developing large-scale software systems.
"Introduction to the Theory of Computation" by Michael Sipser

Finally, explore theoretical computer science concepts. This book covers automata theory, formal languages, computability, and complexity theory, which are important for understanding the theoretical foundations of computer science.
Remember to pace yourself and align your study schedule with your academic coursework. It's also essential to supplement your reading with practice problems, programming exercises, and real-world applications to reinforce your learning. Additionally, stay updated with the latest developments in the field through online resources, journals, and research papers.
